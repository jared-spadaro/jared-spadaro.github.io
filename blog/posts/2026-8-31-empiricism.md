The information landscape of today is filled with misinformation, disinformation, conspiracy theories, verbal take downs, and bold proclamations. It's widely agreed upon that this toxic wasteland of epithets and pejoratives is a major contributing factor to the general decline of our culture and politics. If there's one idea that I wish would become more widespread, it is that the only certainty is uncertainty. The tribalism which defines our time reinforces the idea that every topic of discussion must be met with an unshakeable belief. The desire to be part of a team no doubt plays a significant role in this phenomenon. However, this incentive structure is at cross purposes with the desire for truth. Insofar as one is interested in the truth at all, there is an essential principle governing this quest. To wit: the strength of one's beliefs should be proportional to the strength of the evidence supporting them.

Empiricism is simply the idea that the only legitimate evidence that can be marshalled in support of a given belief is data derived from observation. This data can be measurements given by instruments (as in science) or it can be sensory inputs. The important thing to realize is that although the data itself is the sole unimpeachable source of knowledge, it's functionally unusable in its raw form. It's not until we *construct a story around the data* that it takes on any meaning for us. This crucial step of narrative formation is where things start to go awry. Humans have a bottomless appetite for interpreting data to satisfy their psychological predilections.

It must be admitted that certain topics of discussion lend themselves more readily to this approach than others. Many questions of societal importance simply do not lend themselves to an empirical treatment. Rather, they are questions of *values*. "Should abortion be illegal?" What data can be unearthed which would point us in the direction of the answer? We can categorize questions into two broad camps: questions of truth, and questions of values. In the former, a dispassionate search for the truth is required. In the latter, a political system which allows for the expression of many differing values, and elects representatives to create and enforce a set of laws which embody the intersection of the values that a critical mass of the citizenry can agree upon.

Determining which camp a given question belongs to is not always trivial.
